## Bayes’ Rule

Given a measurement model
$$p(\mathbf{Z}_k|x_k)=
\begin{cases}1-p_{\mathrm{D}}\left(x_k\right) & \text { if } \mathbf{Z}=\emptyset \\ p_{\mathrm{D}}\left(x_k\right) g_k\left(z_k \mid x_k\right) & \text { if } \mathbf{Z}_k=z_k .\end{cases}
$$
Performing an update follows Bayes’ Rule for a single object with measurements over $k$ instances, $\text{posterior}\propto \text{prior}\times\text{likelihood}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
p\left(x_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{1: k}\right) & \propto p\left(x_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{1: k-1}\right) p\left(\mathbf{Z}_k \mid x_k\right) \\
& = \begin{cases}p\left(x_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{1: k-1}\right)\left(1-p_{\mathrm{D}}\left(x_k\right)\right) & \text { if } \mathbf{Z}_k=\emptyset \\
p\left(x_k \mid \mathbf{Z}_{1: k-1}\right) p_{\mathrm{D}}\left(x_k\right) g_k\left(z_k \mid x_k\right) & \text { if } \mathbf{Z}_k=z_k .\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $p_D(x_k)$ is the probability of detecting the object.

## Measurement model

**Objective:**
- We need a $p(Z_k|x_k)$, needed in the updated step, with $Z_k=[O_k; C_k]$ differentiating measurements of Objects and Clutter measurements in a matrix.

**Object detections:** The object is detected with probability $p_D(x_k)$, and, if detected, generates $o_k\sim g_k(o_k|x_k)$.

**Clutter detections:** The number of clutter measurements is $m^c_k\sim \text{Poisson}(\bar \lambda_c)$, and the clutter measurements, $c^1_k,c^2_k,\dots,c^{m^c_k}_k$ are i.i.d. $c^j_k\sim f_c(c^j_k)$.

The *challenges* that arise:
1. The width of $Z_k$ is random
2. It is unknown which detection in $Z_k$ is an object detection or clutter

From here we omit the time index $k$.

### Data associations

To describe the data association, we use ‘Hypotheses‘:
$$\theta=\begin{cases}i>0 \quad & \text{if } z^i \text{ is an object detection} \\ 0 \quad & \text{if object is undetected} \end{cases}$$
Suppose we have $Z_k=[z^1,z^2,z^3]$, if $\theta=2$ then $z^2$ is an object detection whereas the other 2 indices are clutter. If the *data association variable(hypotheses)* $\theta=0$ then the object is undetected and all three measurements are **clutter** detections.

**FINDING** $p(Z, m, \theta \mid x)$

If $\theta=0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(Z \mid m, \theta, x) & =\prod_{i=1}^m f_c\left(z^i\right) \\
p(\theta, m \mid x) & =\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right) \operatorname{Po}\left(m ; \bar{\lambda}_c\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
which implies that
$$
p(Z, m, \theta \mid x)=\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right) \operatorname{Po}\left(m ; \bar{\lambda}_c\right) \prod_{i=1}^m f_c\left(z^i\right) .
$$
\item[-] Using $\operatorname{Po}(m ; \bar{\lambda})=\frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right) \bar{\lambda}_c^m}{m!}$ and $f_c(z)=\lambda_c(z) / \bar{\lambda}_c$ :
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(Z, m, \theta \mid x) & =\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right) \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right) \bar{\lambda}_c^m}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \frac{\lambda_c\left(z^i\right)}{\bar{\lambda}_c} \\
& =\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right) \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right)}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_c\left(z^i\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\theta=1, \ldots, m$,
$$
p(Z \mid m, \theta, x)=g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right) \prod_{\substack{i=1 \\ i \neq \theta}}^N f_c\left(z^i\right)=g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right) \frac{\prod_{i=1}^m f_c\left(z^i\right)}{f_c\left(z^\theta\right)}
$$
$$
p(\theta, m \mid x)=P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \operatorname{Po}\left(m-1 ; \bar{\lambda}_c\right) \frac{1}{m}
$$
which implies that
$$
p(Z, m, \theta \mid x)=P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \operatorname{Po}\left(m-1 ; \bar{\lambda}_c\right) \frac{1}{m} \frac{g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{f_c\left(z^\theta\right)} \prod_{i=1}^m f_c\left(z^i\right) .
$$
\item[-] Using $\mathrm{Po}(m-1 ; \bar{\lambda})=\frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right) \bar{\lambda}_c^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}$ and $f_c(z)=\lambda_c(z) / \bar{\lambda}_c$ :
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(Z, m, \theta \mid x) & =P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right) \bar{\lambda}_c^{m-1}}{(m-1)!} \frac{1}{m} \frac{\bar{\lambda}_c g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{\lambda_c\left(z^\theta\right)} \prod_{i=1}^m \frac{\lambda_c\left(z^i\right)}{\bar{\lambda}_c} \\
& =P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \frac{g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{\lambda_c\left(z^\theta\right)} \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right)}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_c\left(z^i\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
**Example**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left\{\begin{array}{l}
Z=\left\{z^1, z^2\right\} \\
\theta=m=2
\end{array}\right. \\
\Rightarrow & p(Z \mid m, \theta, x) \\
& =g_k\left(z^2 \mid x\right) f_c\left(z^1\right) \\
& =g_k\left(z^2 \mid x\right) \frac{f_c\left(z^1\right) f_c\left(z^2\right)}{f_c\left(z^2\right)} \\
& =g_k\left(z^2 \mid x\right) \frac{\prod_{i=1}^m f_c\left(z^i\right)}{f_c\left(z^\theta\right)}
\end{aligned}
$$
**Putting these 2 equations together** the case where we have detected and object and other values are clutter, and the case where all measurements are clutter. Still focussing on *Single Object Tracking* 

**Measurement model:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\begin{aligned}
p(Z \mid x) & =\sum_{\theta=0}^m p(Z, \theta \mid x) \\
& =\overbrace{\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right) \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right)}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_c\left(z^i\right)}^{\theta=0}+\sum_{\theta=1}^m P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \frac{g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{\lambda_c\left(z^\theta\right)} \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right)}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_c\left(z^i\right) \\
& =\left[\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right)+P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \sum_{\theta=1}^m \frac{g_k\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{\lambda_c\left(z^\theta\right)}\right] \frac{\exp \left(-\bar{\lambda}_c\right)}{m!} \prod_{i=1}^m \lambda_c\left(z^i\right) .
\end{aligned}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Posterior Density:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(x \mid Z) & \propto p(x) p(Z \mid x) \\
& \propto p(x)\left[\left(1-P^{\mathrm{D}}(x)\right)+P^{\mathrm{D}}(x) \sum_{\theta=1}^m \frac{g\left(z^\theta \mid x\right)}{\lambda_c\left(z^\theta\right)}\right] .
\end{aligned}
$$
Which gives a posterior on the full set of measurements. We want to express it in a **mixture** of densities.

## Single Object tracking Filtering Recursions

Let the sequences of measurements of data association hypotheses up to time $k$ be denoted as:
$$ Z_{1:k}=(Z_1, Z_2, ..., Z_k), \quad \theta_{1:k}=(\theta_1,...,\theta_k)$$
The structure of the posterior density, and specifically the **filtering density** can be written as:
$$p(x_k|Z_{1:k})=\sum\limits_{\theta_{1:k}}p(x_k|Z_{1:k},\theta_{1:k})Pr[\theta_{1:k}|Z_{1:k}]$$
The second term, $Pr[\theta_{1:k}|Z_{1:k}]$ scales the different terms and weights the unlikely sequences of hypotheses. The law of total probability is used to show the factoring in the filtering density:
$$p(x_k|Z_{1:k})=\sum\limits_{\theta_{1:k}}p(x_k,\theta_{1:k}|Z_{1:k})$$
Whereas the **predicted density** can be written as:
$$p(x_{k+1}|Z_{1:k})=\sum\limits_{\theta_{1:k}}p(x_{k+1}|Z_{1:k},\theta_{1:k})Pr[\theta_{1:k}|Z_{1:k}]$$
The data association hypotheses do not change when performing the prediction step, the only differences are that the predicted densities in each term are the densities of $x_{k+1}$. Then $p(x_{k+1}|Z_{1:k},\theta_{1:k})$ is obtained performing the Chapman Kolmogorov equation. The [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U2hab6qrzqw&list=PLadnyz93xCLiHWjLcLFdzc-SidNL1kRF7&index=8&pp=iAQB) clearly shows the intractability of prediction and update recursion for an easy linear low state model, it also shows how to use kalman filtering to estimate the posteriors of each marginal distribution while still obeying Chapman Kolmogorov and Bayes’ Rule.

## Measurement update

Measurement likelihood:
$$p(x_k|Z_k)= \sum\limits_{\theta_k=0}^{m_k}p(Z_k,m_k,\theta_k|x_k)$$
Posterior Density, omitting time indices $k$ again
$$p(x|Z)\propto p(x)p(Z|x)$$
In this the prior $p(x)$ is the density **before** obtaining a new measurement $z$.
Which writes to$$ \sum\limits_{\theta=0}^m p(x)p(Z,m,\theta|x).$$
Then the desired form:
$$p(x|Z)=\sum\limits_{\theta=0}^m w_{\theta}p_\theta (x)$$
where:$$\begin{cases}{w}_\theta 
\quad & \text{is a pmf } (Pr[\theta|Z]) \text{ non-negative and sums to 1}\\ p_\theta(x) & \text{is a pdf } (p(x|\theta,Z)) \text{ non-negative and integrates to 1}\end{cases}$$
